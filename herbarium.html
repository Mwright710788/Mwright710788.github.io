UCO Herbarium ETL Pipeline

This project conducted a detailed examination of the University of Central Oklahoma’s Herbarium digitized collection, which consists of approximately 11,000 specimen records dating to the early 1900s. The collection suffers from quality and accuracy issues common to historical digitized collections; thus, much care was taken to preserve as much information as possible while preparing the data for integration within a Geographic Information System.

A Python script was developed that completes all preprocessing functions of the dataset to prepare it for mapping; these functions include addressing geographic precision discrepancies, performing location quality control checks, removing certain plant species from public facing deliverables, joining the data with the Oklahoma Biodiversity Information System Tracking List [OBIS, 2026], and aggregating data counts. Log files are generated throughout to flag any discovered discrepancies. Once the script has fully executed, three output files are generated that are ready for mapping into the GIS system.

The analysis results showed that, as the client suspected, there is an extreme sampling bias towards areas of the state that are in proximity to the University of Central Oklahoma and its satellite office; additionally, the digitized collection is heavily skewed towards 15% of all plant families in the state of Oklahoma, with these 26 plant families comprising 78% of the entire collection.

Lastly, the Oklahoma Biodiversity Information System was successfully integrated into the analysis with approximately 1% of the herbarium’s collection retaining a state threat ranking based on the NatureServe ranking system. While vulnerable species representation is sparse, this GIS system has the capability of being updated at the client’s discretion. This integration has the potential to aid the herbarium in threatened species and appropriate conservation measures as the number of threatened records continues to accumulate.

The GIS system created for the University of Central Oklahoma effectively answers all questions outlined by the client and positions them to update the system easily as their collection efforts and the digitization process continue.
